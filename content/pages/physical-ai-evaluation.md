<section class="ev-split"><div class="ev-text"><span class="ev-num">01 · The problem</span><h2>Why physical AI evaluation is hard</h2><p class="ev-lead">Different environments, different architectures, tiny samples. Most claims you read are unfalsifiable.</p><p>The question is how good these models actually are on real commercial tasks – not demos, not simulation, not success rates on 10 tries.</p><div class="ev-stats"><div class="ev-stat"><b>~20</b><span>trials in a robot eval, with a single success rate and no confidence intervals</span></div><div class="ev-stat"><b>4 → 3</b><span>four reasonable scoring choices on the same data produce three different "winners"</span></div></div><p>At that scale, today's top models are statistically indistinguishable – most reported differences are noise.</p><p>We know sim and real scores correlate. What we don't know is how much a sim rank is worth on real hardware, and we don't think anyone does.</p></div></section>

<section class="ev-split"><div class="ev-text"><span class="ev-num">02 · Our method</span><h2>How we do it</h2><p class="ev-lead">We test robot AI models on real robots in our lab. Real hardware. Blind runs. Public data.</p><ol class="ev-list"><li>One real robot, a Franka FR3.</li><li>The operator is blind to the model, and every try is on video.</li><li>We measure what operations people care about: units per hour and time between failures.</li><li>A distributional methodology that separates close models with <b>~30x fewer trials</b>.</li><li>The benchmark, the dataset and the code are public.</li></ol><p>See the results on <a href="https://phail.ai">PhAIL</a>, our public leaderboard for physical AI. Every run on it has full video and telemetry. To test your model without publishing the results, ask us for a private evaluation.</p></div></section>
