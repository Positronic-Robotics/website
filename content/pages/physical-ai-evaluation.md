<section class="ev-split"><div class="ev-text"><span class="ev-num">01 · The problem</span><h2>Why physical AI evaluation is hard</h2><p class="ev-lead">Different environments, different architectures, tiny samples. Most claims you read are unfalsifiable.</p><p>The question is how good these models actually are on real commercial tasks – not demos, not simulation, not success rates on 10 tries.</p><div class="ev-stats"><div class="ev-stat"><b>~20</b><span>trials in a robot eval, with a single success rate and no confidence intervals</span></div><div class="ev-stat"><b>4 → 3</b><span>four reasonable scoring choices on the same data produce three different "winners"</span></div></div><p>At that scale, today's top models are statistically indistinguishable – most reported differences are noise.</p><p>We know sim and real scores correlate. What we don't know is how much a sim rank is worth on real hardware, and we don't think anyone does.</p></div><figure class="ev-clip ev-sticky"><video data-src="/media/eval-lab-4.mp4" poster="/media/eval-lab-4-poster.jpg" muted loop playsinline preload="none" width="720" height="1280" aria-label="A robot gripper, a fork and a red bowl"></video></figure></section>

Real hardware. Blind runs.
